Curatorial Essay

Why Waste a Tree on Anything Less?

In the summer of 2006, cartoonist K.C. Green confronted the foundational anxiety of all visual art: trees die so that human beings may draw things on paper. Determined to honor that arboreal sacrifice with utmost solemnity, Green’s protagonist pressed pencil to pulp and produced the stupidest conceivable configuration of lines.

Where Marcel Duchamp merely signed a porcelain urinal in 1917, Green achieved true mise-en-abyme—an anatomical recursion wherein subject and punchline collapse into a single stoic silhouette. For two decades, internet artisans hid this figure at the end of forty-panel sagas, encrypted QR codes, and corporate slide decks, reminding civilization that vigilance is futile.
